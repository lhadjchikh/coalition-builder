Most endorsements are obvious in both directions. This page is for the rest.

## Approve when

- The person or organization appears legitimate and identifiable
- The email domain is consistent with who they say they are
- Any statement is on topic and professionally worded
- If they are endorsing on behalf of an organization, their role plausibly carries
  the authority to do so

## Reject when

- It is spam, an advertisement, or unrelated promotional content
- The name is obviously fake or a placeholder
- The statement contains profanity, slurs, or personal attacks
- The organization name looks fabricated — "Business LLC," "Company Inc"
- It is part of an obvious duplicate or coordinated batch
- It makes claims that are plainly false

## Ask before deciding when

- A high-profile organization or an elected official endorses. Worth flagging
  regardless of how legitimate it looks.
- Someone claims to speak for a large organization and you cannot verify the
  connection.
- The statement is on topic but politically sensitive, or takes a position beyond
  what the campaign actually says.
- Someone asks to withdraw or delete their endorsement.
- You get a legal, press, or privacy inquiry.
- **Anything you are unsure about.** Leaving something in the queue for a day costs
  nothing. Approving a fraudulent organizational endorsement costs the organization
  its credibility.

## The default when you're stuck

**Don't approve.** An endorsement sitting in the queue is harmless. A bad
endorsement on the public site is not.

## A note on other people's words

Fixing an obvious typo in someone's statement is fine. Changing what they said is
not — it is their words, submitted under their name, and we publish it as an
endorsement from them. If a statement needs more than a typo fix, it is a
conversation with {{ supervisor_contact }}, not an edit.
